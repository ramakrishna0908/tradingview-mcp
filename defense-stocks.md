  **Strait of Hormuz** (~20% of world oil shipments); Iran hit US facilities in Jordan. Ukrainian drone strike
  hit Russia's Ust-Luga export terminal.
- **Oil >$90 Brent / ~$88 WTI.** USO +5.5% to a 21-day high on 1.71x volume.
- **Global bond selloff.** US 10Y **4.788%** (20-month high, highest since Jan 2025); Japan 10Y 3% (first since
  1996); UK 30Y gilt 5.89% (highest since 1998). Euro-area CPI 3.3%, energy +14.3%. Market pricing **Fed hike** risk.

**So the defense primes fell as long-duration, bond-proxy cash flows in a yield shock — NOT because a war
premium was unwinding.** This matters: the sector is oversold *into an escalating conflict*, which is a very
different setup from oversold into peace. Same shock explains AXON -8.5%, PLTR's flow collapse, NVDA failing
to hold a +106%-revenue earnings gap, TSLA's failed cloud breakout, and IWM below its lower band.

## 📝 Correction log

A WebSearch result describing a US-Iran **ceasefire/de-escalation** was **stale** and produced a wrong initial
sector thesis ("war premium unwinding"). The **oil tape contradicted it** — USO +5.5% to 21-day highs is
impossible under de-escalation — which exposed the error and led to the correct read (re-escalation + rates shock).

**Lesson:** cross-check a macro narrative against a price that *must* move with it (oil for geopolitics,
yields/TLT for rates) before building a sector call on a search snippet.
